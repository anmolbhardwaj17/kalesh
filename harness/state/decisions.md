## 2026-11-11: Bake driver, stages and pins (P1-007)
Decision: `tools/bake/main.ts` is the one bake driver (`npm run bake -- <stage>`), with `tools/bake/sources/` the only other code that touches files or the network (`purity.test.ts`). A stage is an entry in `districts.json` `stages` (outline, margin, data folder, sources); the pilot is `districts/pilot_centre.geojson`, 21.8 km2, margin 400 m, one box extract per source in `data/pilot_centre/`. Every request goes through one `Net` seam, so tests count requests with a fake. A pinned file is reused only when its sha256 matches and its pinned box holds the stage box; a changed local file stops the bake (never overwritten without `--force`); `--offline` never fetches. GLO-30 stays one shared pin per 1 degree tile (`glo30`). Node typings come from `tools/bake/sources/node.d.ts`, not `@types/node`. `rand.ts` is sfc32 seeded by splitmix32 from `seedFor(tileX, tileZ, parcelId, stream)`; its first values are pinned in a test, because a change reshuffles every baked city.
Why: TECH.md "World pipeline": deterministic, pinned sources, stop before tiles on a failed invariant; a fake network proves the second run makes no request.
By: builder, P1-007.

## 2026-11-11: Layout, world grid and recentre in the bake; island stitch code retired (P1-008)
Decision: `tools/bake/layout/` owns the layout. A block's offset is always computed by the signed method (the numbers in `districts.json` are a record, checked). Rule R-STITCH (01 data, 02 translation only, 03 centre pairs, 04 far blocks, 05 block gaps of 250 m, 06 extent) runs in every bake before the first step and stops it. One 250 m world grid (`grid.ts`), tile ids from world metres. Far block frame origins are checked OSM places: Qutub Minar, DLF Promenade, Terminal 3, DLF Cyber Hub; their outlines are compact drafts (1.7 to 2.9 km2). The airport block leaves Aerocity to the airport road corridor. The recentre step subtracts one vector, a whole number of tiles, and puts the world origin on the centre of the smallest circle round the map, not on the centre of the map box. The reach limit is 8,192 m. The stitch prototype is retired: needed parts moved (ARCHITECTURE.md row "Stitch prototype, retired"), the bench driver, plan and scene removed, the island fields taken out of `districts.json`.
Why: measured with every planned block placed, the map box is 13.03 by 13.37 km and its centre leaves Cyber City 9.0 km out; the smallest circle has a radius of 7.98 km (8.03 km after snapping to tiles), inside the band where a 32-bit float steps by 0.49 mm, the band FP1 measured at 7 km. Aerocity at true scale lands on the Vasant Kunj malls, because the two block origins are only half their real distance apart. A whole-tile vector means no tile is cut again and overrides files are renamed, not re-filed.

## 2026-11-12: Lane model for the pilot centre (P1-012)
Decision: `tools/bake/roads/network.ts` builds the lane model of the whole repaired block graph as the bake step `lanes` (after `repair`), on the prototype's modules. It never moves a centre line. Readings where the design leaves a gap:
- Whole-street inference (`street.ts`): one count per edge. A spec, a tag (by tagged length) or a logged lane repair is kept; other edges take the nearest such count of their street, then the other carriageway's, then the class default. A street runs on through a one-way split along its main line whatever the names say. A paired carriageway may not exceed the room beside its pair.
- At a split zone lanes are conserved: a branch with only a default count carries what the road before can feed; a road too short for its taper takes the zone's first section whole, or one extra lane at most; if that is still not enough the tagged branch brings fewer lanes; if nothing fits the split is built as a junction. Where a street changes count at a junction the taper lies on the side that has room.
- Not zones: entries and exits of roundabouts (give-way junctions), splits within 18 m of a junction node (cluster rule), splits whose branches do not come a nose gap apart within their own edge. Each is listed in `forks` with its reason.
- Junctions: nodes joined by an edge of 18 m or less are one junction (the number of rule R-JUNC-03); an edge left under 1.5 m between its cuts, or blocking a 4 m turn, becomes part of the junction.
- Turn rules (`connect.ts`): no turn back into the same arm; one-way rules; a turn over 135 degrees only as a U-turn between the two carriageways of a street and only into a lane it reaches on 4 m; restrictions ("no", "only") as input. An arm with no other way keeps its least sharp turn. The pilot extract has no restriction relations and no `turn:lanes` (Overpass query fetches multipolygons only).
- Galis: a driving lane under 2.4 m exists only as the one shared lane of a gali (1.8 m at least).
- Stop lines sit 4 m before the cut where an arm has a crossing (signals, a tagged crossing within 30 m, or a main road with footpaths), else on the cut.
Measured on the pilot centre: 5,823 strips, 101,851 stations, 9,150 driving lanes, 141 zones (66 diverges, 75 merges), 138 forks built as junctions (68 roundabout, 32 beside a junction, 35 branches too close, 3 too short), 2,827 junctions, 21,140 paths for 19,113 arm pairs, continuity breaks 0, tapers out of limits 4 of 87 (all four only shift lane widths), hero mismatches 0 over 6,492 comparisons, model built in 2 to 7 s, same hash twice.
Open (not met): 4 arm pairs turn on less than 4 m (1.68 to 3.39 m; kept so the lane is not cut off); the ITO site of the bench gives 8 zones and 2 forks of its 10 splits; strips lie on each other in plan (ITO box 101 m2, pilot 4,917 m2 in 494 pairs) because the repair's shift keeps nodes and fades at edge ends and no rule keeps split branches apart. The lane model reports these, it does not hide them.
Why: MAP_REPAIR.md "Lane-level road model"; the old game's overlapping roads came from building each way alone; pillar 2 needs lanes that join over flyovers.
By: builder, P1-012.

## 2026-11-12: Elevation solver (P1-013)
Decision: the production elevation solver is `tools/bake/elevation/`, the bake step `elevation` after `lanes`. It keeps the prototype's cost (near the ground model, smooth, carriageway pairs level, junctions gentle) and changes five things.
- Sparse: the unknowns are ordered by minimum degree and factored along the elimination tree (`sparse.ts`, our own, no package). Pilot: 38,539 unknowns, factor 253,177 values, about 40 ms per factor.
- Profiles are chains, not strips (`profile.ts`): strips that run on into each other share knots every 12 m and the height between knots is a quadratic B-spline. A limit on every knot interval (grade) and every knot (vertical curve) then holds at every point, and a joint has no step and no kink. At a split the chain goes on into one branch; the other branch's first two knots follow the chain's height and grade.
- Limits are never traded against the cost (`multipliers.ts`): method of multipliers, a damped Newton step per round. Grade, curve and junction slope have no cap; clearances stop at a cap, so they give way last and are listed (`unmet`) if they do. Map edge ties are stiff springs to the ground model (weight 10,000), not fixed heights: a clearance next to the edge moves the tie and the record says so.
- Clearance is held wherever two surfaces of different levels lie on each other in plan (`crossings.ts`), not only where centre lines cross: a road that runs along under a deck counts. New rules: deck over water (2.5 m under the deck above the lowest ground model height in the water), railway lines (a fixed datum: the ground model, or 4.5 m above it for a railway bridge over a road; 6.5 m over the rail, 5.5 m under a railway bridge), tunnel cover (6.7 m; a tunnel tag at layer 0 is a building passage and stays at grade).
- Second phase after the first converges: a ceiling on every deck at the highest place a clearance needs it (without it a deck arched 2.5 m over ITO between its two ramp supports), and the crossfall limit (4 percent) where both carriageways came out at grade. A ground level road under a deck is held to the ground 200 times harder than a station, so ramps start earlier first and the road under goes down only then (`sunk`).
Measured on the pilot (2026-11-12): solve 9.8 s (32 rounds, 230 factors), 0 steps at 512 joints, 0 grade and 0 curve violations (94,599 segments, steepest 8.00 against 8), least headroom 5.50 m at 77 road crossings, 5.51 m under 111 railway bridge crossings, 6.57 m over 69 rail crossings, 2.83 m over water, 0 crossfall over 4 percent, 36 structures (17 deck, 11 tunnel, 8 cut; 18,352 m), two runs identical, 5 of 169 map edge ties moved by a clearance.
Not met: ITO within 0.3 m of Phase 0 on every strip. The decks and merge zones are within 0.13 m and the median over 463 points is 0.15 m, but the ramps differ: loop ramps 0.31 and 0.65 m, north approach 0.54 m, south approaches 1.04 and 1.46 m (ours higher mid ramp). The Phase 0 site had one 8-arm junction whose circle held everything at 7 m over about 200 m; the block lane model has two smaller junctions there, and the ramp shape follows from that. Tried: sharing only the height at a split (no better, 1.32 m). Next to try: compare the two lane models strip by strip at the south approach.
Why: MAP_REPAIR.md "Elevation solver"; the old game's floating and sunk roads came from heights sampled per vertex; pillar 2 (the ITO flyovers).
By: builder, P1-013.

## 2026-11-12: Road and junction mesh (P1-014)
Decision: the production road mesh is `tools/bake/mesh/`, the bake step `mesh` after `elevation`. It reads the lane model and the heights and builds neither again.
- The top surface is a set of plan faces (lane quads, junction surfaces, raised corners) over vertices welded to the millimetre. Watertight is a property of edges: every edge two faces meet along is named by both. T joints are repaired by putting the vertex into the edge; joint rows are pinned onto each other; folds on the inside of tight bends are taken out.
- J3 as built: J1 is the planned ring of `roads/junction.ts` on the strips' own vertices, with the bands drawn in to the trimmed edge widths (`LaneArm.edges`). The polygon set (clipper2-ts) has two roles: a face that lies on a neighbour is cut back to what is free, and a cluster with no usable ring is the union of its lane path corridors and a mouth at every arm (not a polygon of chords: that paved 3.3 ha at Kashmere Gate).
- Kerb faces, band edges and end caps are not placed by rule: a vertical face is drawn wherever two tops that share an edge differ in height. Parapets, walls, undersides, roofs, piers and deck joints exist only on structure stations of the elevation step, and only along edges no other face shares.
- Lane markings are lane UVs (u across the lane, v metres along the road) with a code per lane border (none, dashed, centre, edge); stop lines and zebra crossings are geometry. The stand-in bench scene draws the same codes as strips, because the T1 tile has no UV channel and the road shader is not written.
- Overlap is proven by the polygon union per level sheet and pair by pair at every junction. Levels are the solved station kinds, not the layer tags.
- The road bench scene (`--arch=roads:<site>`) renders the production mesh (four sites); the Phase 0 driver `harness/bench/roads.mjs` no longer feeds it.
Measured on the pilot (2026-11-12): build 6.9 s, 378,753 faces, 1.66 million triangles; 2,775 junctions (2,712 as planned, 63 by the polygon set), 487 faces of 315 junctions cut back; 0 pairs over 0.01 m2 at junctions (13,127 tested, largest 0.0025 m2); open boundary edges 0 (4,723 T joints repaired); parapet triangles at grade 0 of 27,520; piers in a lane 0 of 191; 478,500 kerb faces all 0.15 m; lane UV test passes; 1,326 stop lines on 1,326 arms that stop.
Not met: unjoined ends 6 and floating ends 2 (arms of clusters that span levels or have no room at their mouth; one raised dead end the elevation step lists too); strip on strip 3 pairs, 13.47 m2 (one ramp on fill beside a road at grade, 13.41 m2, which the lanes step cannot see with tag levels); 10 junction surfaces over 3,000 m2. All four come from the lane model's clusters and cuts, not from the mesh.
Why: MAP_REPAIR.md "Junction mesh builder"; the old game's overlapping road meshes, misplaced railings and junctions that did not join each have a count here; the ground (P1-015) is cut to the boundary loops.

## 2026-11-12: The ground is one constrained Delaunay triangulation, our own (P1-015)
Decision: the production ground is `tools/bake/terrain/`, the bake step `terrain` after `mesh`. No package: the constrained Delaunay triangulation is ours (`cdt.ts`, `cdtsegments.ts`, `predicates.ts`, about 600 lines), so nothing was installed.
- One triangulation for the whole stage, not one per block. Its segments are the free edges of the road mesh's own faces, the building footprints, water, open land, the stage outline and the lines of the 250 m world grid. A triangle learns what it lies in by counting across the labelled edges from outside the stage: road (nothing drawn), footprint (a pad, material `plot`), water (a flat surface over a bed), open land (`lawn`) or ground. Because the tile lines are edges, no triangle crosses a tile line and both sides of a seam use the same vertices.
- Coordinates are whole quarter millimetres, so the turn test is exact in doubles; the circle test falls back to BigInt when its sign is not certain. A vertex within 2 mm of a segment becomes part of it; two segments that cross get a vertex on the crossing.
- The terrain step reads the free edges itself and does not use the mesh's boundary loops, for two reasons the mesh's `cut` code cannot say: a tunnel ramp that has no roof yet must cut the ground (the mesh says "not at all" for every tunnel face), and a junction on the ground level sheet with one arm on a deck or in a tunnel lies on the ground (the mesh says "not at all" there too; 0.06 km2 of road on the pilot). An edge of such a junction more than 2.2 m off the ground model is met like a wall.
- Heights: a vertex on a road edge at grade takes the road's own height there. Every other vertex takes the ground model plus what the road edges within 30 m spread (the road was solved up to 3.1 m off the model on the pilot). At a wall the ground keeps its own height, never above a ramp, never under a cut road. Where two road faces of different heights end at one vertex the ground has a height per triangle corner and an upright face closes the step.
- The points inside blocks are a staggered 12.5 m lattice on the world grid (the height grid of open land is this lattice), and no segment is longer than 6.25 m.
- Collider chunks leave out triangles under a square millimetre (`roads/mesh.ts colliderChunks`): a needle of the ground has no area as 32-bit floats. The road bench's probe points moved 5 cm off the station line: a ray straight down the edge between two collider chunks can pass between the two bodies (one probe at Connaught Place did).
Measured on the pilot (2026-11-12): one build 5.2 s (twice in the bake for the same-hash check), 680,136 points, 1.44 million triangles, 1,068,742 drawn in 389 tiles; road to ground gap at most 0.0015 m on 574,330 points; 0 ground vertices above a road, 0 into a deck slab; 1.37 percent of 771,363 ground triangles under 1 degree (the prototype: 80 percent); 55,000 seam edges, 0 differ; ground, footprints and road faces miss the outline area by 0.002 percent; 290,930 sample points, 0 in a hole.
Not met, listed in health.json: 642 m of road edge has no ground along it because another road face lies there (the lane model's overlapping clusters) and 2 m for no known reason; 34 of 36,754 crossings could not get a vertex and went through the nearer end of the crossed edge.
Why: MAP_REPAIR.md "Terrain that fits the roads" (TF2); the old game's ground and roads were two surfaces that disagreed, here the ground ends where the road begins.
By: builder, P1-015.
