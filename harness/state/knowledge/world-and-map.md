# world-and-map

One line per gotcha.

- src/geo/projection.ts: z is metres SOUTH of the origin (north is -Z), so `geoToWorld` returns negative z for anything north of India Gate; landmarks: Connaught Place park about (-1246, -2062), Red Fort about (+1130, -4800).
- The series projection round trip is about 0.4 mm, not exact: assert round trips under 1 cm / 1e-7 degrees, not 1e-9.
- Godot `Vector2`/`Vector3` are 32 bit floats in the standard build (0.4 mm error at 25 km); `game/geo/projection.gd` returns plain float Dictionaries. Do geo maths in floats, convert to Vector3 only to place nodes.
- `Projection` is a Godot builtin type: a preload const named Projection fails to parse. The port is preloaded as `Geo`.
- Stand-in scenes (game/scenes/standin/): GDScript inner classes cannot call the outer script's static funcs, so the Sink (geo.gd) reaches primitives through `const P := preload("prims.gd")`; geo.gd extends prims.gd so `Geo.extrude()` and `Geo.SEG_ROUND` work outside.
- Godot front faces are clockwise: for an outward face (b - a).cross(c - a) points inward, and a closed outward solid has negative signed volume (checked against BoxMesh in test_standin.gd). prims.gd tri() winds from the normal, so pass the outward normal, never hand-order vertices.
- A Kit.at(p, yaw) frame faces -Z at yaw 0; to face +X use yaw -PI/2, to face -X use +PI/2 (rotating -Z by yaw gives (-sin, 0, -cos)).
- Bake code (`tools/bake/`, P0-018): Node 22 runs the `.ts` files directly, so imports between them carry the `.ts` ending (both tsconfigs have `allowImportingTsExtensions`), no enums or parameter properties, and no Node types (`types: []`): file and network work stays in `.mjs` drivers (`harness/bench/pilot_data/`). Geometry is in district metres, origin at the area centre (`projector(area.centre)`).
- Source fetch (`fetch.mjs`): Overpass answers 406 to curl and Mozilla client names, a plain custom name works; never query `nwr` with `>` (boundary and route relations pull in the country), ask for nodes, ways and tagged multipolygon relations. FABDEM is a 1.9 GB zip per 10 degrees: one tile is read out with byte ranges. GEDTM30 is one global file: geotiff.js `fromUrl` reads a window. Google Open Buildings cell 391 is 6.7 GB of gzip (17 minutes here): streamed once and hashed, only pilot rows kept. `fetch failed` on a licence page is often a passing network fault: run `--only licences` again before calling it unreachable.
- Licences checked 2026-11-11 (table in `harness/bench/pilot_data/licences.mjs`): Microsoft footprints are CDLA Permissive 2.0 now, not ODbL; GEDTM30 v1.2 is CC BY 4.0 but its record says "testing purposes"; FABDEM is CC BY-NC-SA (never shipped). The ASI Delhi list page has no list in its HTML: ASI ids come through Wikidata P1371 (160 ids).
- Height data in the pilot: GEDTM30 read cell by cell is noisy (about 1 m between neighbours, steps to 2.7 m in 15 m along a road); smoothed over 60 m it is the smoothest source. GLO-30 needs the ground filter (`fitGround`). All three files are EGM2008 heights; they differ from each other by 1 to 2 m, so only relative relief is trusted.
- OSM in the pilot: lanes on under 9 percent of road ways, width on under 4 percent, building height or levels on 4 to 19 percent. Rajiv Chowk station is a 25,000 m2 `building=train_station` on layer -2: `buildMap` leaves out footprints below ground. The CP radials are true one-way streets (R-ROAD-04 flags them for review, never auto).
- Overture buildings (BS1) drop the OSM tags wikidata, historic, amenity, religion and addr: the landmark join reads OSM directly. Overture leaves out about half of Google's footprints in dense blocks (the ones sitting on roads); our own merge keeps them and gets 1,858 footprints with a road centre line through them in Chandni Chowk against 7.
- Tiles: the codec is `tools/bake/tile/codec.ts` (moved from `tile.ts` in P1-016; stand-ins in `tile/standin.ts`, `.glb` in `tile/glb.ts`). Triangles wind clockwise from the outward normal (Godot) and flip for `.glb`. The T1 file and its GDScript reader (`game/bench/tiles/tile_t1.gd`) are tied by the fixture `harness/data/fixtures/tile_t1_sample.json`, now a production tile from `tile/sample.ts` (`node tools/fixtures/gen_tile_fixture.mjs` after any format or writer change).
- `data/` is gitignored except the pin list `data/SOURCES.json`. Overrides (the three bench samples and the inspector's picks) live in `harness/data/overrides/<tileX>_<tileZ>.json`, filed under the world tile of the patch anchor; a district folder there makes the bake stop.
- Road model prototype (`tools/bake/roads/`, P0-019): OSM centre lines are not lane accurate. Three patterns broke a naive build at ITO and each has a step now: the two carriageways of a street drawn closer than their lanes are wide (`fit.ts pushApart`, up to 2.2 m), a slip road drawn as a short diagonal from the main line's node (`limitDivergence`, up to 9.2 m), and every edge smoothed on its own, which kinks the line at a node (`graph.ts` gives edges that run on into each other one tangent). Rebuild a plan's `t1` after any step that changes an edge's curve: its length changes.
- Split zones (`lanes.ts planSplit`): the stretch from a split node to the nose is one strip with both branches' lanes; the lane group boundary must fade with the separation itself (`1 - sep / half`), not with distance, or the lanes that run straight on wobble by half a metre. A zone ends exactly on both branches' own centre lines.
- The road mesh must be watertight for TF2: the ground is cut to the boundary loops of the at-grade road triangles (edges used once), so a T joint anywhere (a kerb end beside a junction edge, a band laid along a strip edge) breaks the loops. Junction rings therefore take every cut line vertex of their arms, and a median nose kerb sits inside the junction ring. `checks.mesh.openBoundaryEdges` is the proof (0).
- Ground under a ramp: a ramp on fill cuts the ground (it ends at the foot of the ramp's walls, at ground model height); only a deck lets the ground run on under it. Ground that ends at the road's own height under a ramp foot pokes through the ramp.
- `tri.ts` is ear clipping: correct area and every boundary vertex kept, but 80 percent of the ground triangles are needles under one degree. Give the stand-in ground one up normal or the needles show as streaks. Production needs a constrained Delaunay with interior points.
- T1 placements are 12 floats per instance with the origin in places 3, 7 and 11 of each twelve: shift by the tile origin with `i % 12`, not `i % 4` alone (NaN origins draw nothing and raise no error).
- New npm packages: `npm install` of a new dependency (Clipper2, earcut) was refused by the permission system in the P0-019 run. Do not work around it: ask the owner, or write the piece (the road prototype has its own triangulator and an overlap check on triangle pairs).

- Tile cutter (`tools/bake/tileset.ts`, from the stitch bench): tiles are 250 m of the one world grid with vertices relative to the tile (`TileSet`); a triangle goes to the tile of its centre, so a small object that must stay whole (a landmark pin) needs the `anchor` argument, and a big triangle (a landmark roof, the water) reaches 630 m from its tile origin.
- Map edge (`tools/bake/layout/edge.ts`, `closures.ts`, from the stitch bench; used only where the map ends, never between focus districts): a way that starts at a shared node just inside the outline is the road's way out, not a "nick"; dropping it left a road that ended on the line with no port. Where the line crosses a street at an angle its two carriageways become two ports (Vikas Marg at Laxmi Nagar, 27 m apart along the road): draw the line square across big roads.
- Connector ends are authored on the port's centre, and that centre moves when the outline or a clip rule changes (10.7 m after the nick fix in the bench): after any such change update the first or last control point. The three files in `harness/data/connectors/` are `bench_only` (island layout); a production connector names `{block, port}` at each end (`tools/bake/connectors/connectors.ts`).
- Height offsets: one height offset per district could not level every port in the bench (ground inside Chandni Chowk falls 9 m towards the river side), and sampling a height grid far outside its download box returns the clamped edge value. So a block has one ground model, and a far block gets one offset solved over all its joins.
- The lane model has no "deck over water" rule: a bridge-tagged stretch is only freed from the ground, so over a wide river bed the deck is set by its two banks. It also never breaks the grade limit: when a flyover gets too short it starts the ramps earlier and then lowers the road under it. Read `sunk`, `structureM` and the clearance, not grade violations (SC2 sweep in `stitch.json`).
- Projection inside the centre block (one India Gate origin): grid north turns by 0.023 degrees 4.7 km east of the origin (the worst centre pair bearing in R-STITCH), distances hold to 0.00002%. Landmark pins read back from T1 tiles are within 0.006 mm, in the running engine within 0.17 mm at 4 km (a 32-bit float steps by 0.24 mm between 2,048 and 4,096 m, 0.49 mm up to 8,192 m).
- Layout (P1-008, `tools/bake/layout/`): a block's offset is always computed (`blockFrame`: pivot.world + factor x geoToWorld(origin, pivot)); the `offset` numbers in `districts.json` are a record to 0.1 m and R-STITCH-01 fails if one differs by more than 0.06 m. After moving a frame origin, run `blockOffset` and write the new numbers. Focus districts have no frame and no offset: only an outline and a label point.
- Far blocks are true scale inside but sit at half their real distance, so two that are close in reality collide when placed: with facing edges g metres apart in reality and origins d apart, the game gap is g minus d/2 along that line. Keep them compact and put the frame origin near the edge that faces a neighbour. Aerocity (3.6 km east of Terminal 3) lands on the Vasant Kunj malls; a Vasant Kunj drawn out to Aruna Asaf Ali Marg overlaps Mehrauli. R-STITCH-05 checks every pair (250 m).
- Map size (measured P1-008, every planned block placed): box 13.03 by 13.37 km; the smallest circle round it has a radius of 7.98 km, so no origin gets everything under 8 km by more than a few metres; the box centre leaves Cyber City 9.0 km out. The recentre uses the circle's centre snapped to whole tiles (8.03 km). The limit is 8,192 m (float step 0.49 mm up to there). The centre block's real outline is not drawn yet: when it is, this gate may fail and needs a new FP1 measurement or a tighter layout.
- Recentre (`recentre.ts`): the vector must be a whole number of 250 m tiles (it throws otherwise), so tiles keep their vertices and only the index and origin change, and an overrides file is renamed. The vector between two layouts is the difference of the pivot's world position, which every record set carries (`pivotWorld`). `smallestCircle` uses no random step (the purity test bans Math.random).
- World grid (`layout/grid.ts`): tile ids come from world metres (`<tileX>_<tileZ>`, `-0` never written). `tile.ts` `tileOf`, `overrides.ts` `overrideTile` and `tileset.ts` call it; `tools/inspector/lib.ts` `worldTile` is a copy of the rule (the page loads lib.ts with no imports) held in step by `grid.test.ts`.
- Overpass on 2026-11-11 evening: overpass-api.de answered 502 and 504 for an hour; `https://maps.mail.ru/osm/tools/overpass/api/interpreter` answered small box queries after a few tries. Give every request a timeout (one mirror held a request open for 10 minutes). `out tags geom` gives way geometry without node ids; `out center` gives the centre of a way's box, not its centroid.
- Map inspector (P1-001, P1-011, `tools/inspector/`): run it with `node tools/inspector/serve.mjs` (port 4490) or through the dashboard at /inspector/ (restart a running dashboard to get the route and the sidebar link). It reads the bake's report folder `harness/reports/map/` (`issues.json`, `context.json`, `tiles/index.json`; no adapter any more): run `npm run bake -- pilot --stage repair --offline` first. `page.test.mjs` uses that report, or makes one with an offline bake into a temp folder, and skips with a reason when the pilot data, Chromium or Godot are missing.
- Inspector page: KTL1 tiles wind triangles clockwise (Godot), three.js wants counter clockwise, so `addTile` swaps two corners per triangle; with DoubleSide and the file winding the lighting flips dark. Headless Chromium (`harness/bin/browser.mjs launch`) draws the WebGL page fine; `preserveDrawingBuffer` is on so screenshots are not blank. The top view is the perspective camera straight down with the camera a hair south of its target (north up, no flip); "Open in game" calls `teleport([x, 0, z])` on the game's debug port (default 47181, env HARNESS_GAME_PORT) and reads the player back.
- Bake driver (P1-007): `npm run bake -- pilot --stage sources` (add `--offline` to prove no request is made). A pin is reused only when its sha256 matches and its pinned `box` holds the stage box, so moving the pilot outline past its 400 m margin refetches about 75 MB (OSM 23 MB, Overture 51 MB through DuckDB in about 3 minutes). A local file that no longer matches its pin stops the bake; `--force` fetches again.
- Bake typings: there is no `@types/node`. `tools/bake/sources/node.d.ts` declares only the Node functions the drivers use (add one there when needed); `fetch`, `console`, `process`, `setTimeout` are reached through `globalThis as unknown as {...}` because the lib is ES2022 only. `purity.test.ts` fails any bake file outside `main.ts` and `sources/` that imports node:fs/net/http, calls fetch or Math.random.
- Overpass answered 504 three times in a row on a second pilot box query 5 minutes after the first: `readOsm` then tries the mirror list (`OVERPASS_MIRRORS`). The pilot box (36 km2) is 220k elements, 23 MB; multipolygon relations are fetched without recursion (a river relation would pull in its whole length).
- Pilot places read from OSM (P1-007): Connaught Place Central Park centroid 28.6328, 77.2197 (not the CP frame origin 28.6315, 77.2167); GB Road is Shraddhanand Road, from Ajmeri Gate (28.6462, 77.2236) north-west, then due north along lon 77.2185 to 28.662, so a straight north-west outline edge cuts it off; the Red Fort complex reaches 28.6624 N (Salimgarh); the Yamuna west bank at the ITO bridge is at lon 77.2533.
- Block graph (P1-009, `tools/bake/graph/`): the pilot builds in about 0.9 s (4,965 nodes, 6,677 edges, 194 map edge ends, 706 carriageway pairs, 34,591 footprints); `npm run bake -- pilot --stage graph --offline`. Pairing by name alone fails in Delhi: halves are spelled apart ("Inderprastha" and "Indraprastha", "Tilak Marg" and "Tilak Road"), unnamed (Mathura Road along Pragati Maidan), or named differently ("Shivaji Flyover" and "Maharaja Ranjeet Singh Marg", "Chelmsford Road" and "Babu Ram Solanki Marg"): those pair only when tight (16 m) at one level, marked `namesDiffer`. Names with different numbers never agree (Radial Road 3 and 4 are one edit apart).
- `Sketch.way([[x, n], ...])` makes a new node per point, so a ring drawn that way is not closed by id: reuse the first node id for areas (water, parks) in tests.
- The prototype site graph (`roads/graph.ts`) now cuts and joins through `graph/topology.ts`; the road bench numbers (`roads_bake.json`, 285 values) were identical after the move. Any change to `topology.ts` changes both graphs.
- Rule engine and auto-repair (P1-010, `tools/bake/rules/`, `tools/bake/repair/`): `npm run bake -- pilot --stage repair --offline` (about 20 s; all 40 rules take 0.5 to 0.9 s on the raw graph, the repair with its passes 6 s). Rules only read: a new rule returns issues from `ctx.issue()` with proposals from `ctx.proposal(action, params, confidence, measure, ...)`, and `repair/bounds.ts` alone turns the measure into low impact or not. Params stay flat (strings, numbers, booleans): `repair/apply.ts` works the geometry out again from them.
- Edges are whole chains between junctions with the tags of their longest way piece, so a lane count change inside a chain does not exist at edge level: lane rules see changes only at nodes with three or more ends or at class joints. In a test put a side street on the node to cut the street there (`street()` in `rules.test.ts`).
- `ctx.through(end)` returns the only other end at a node with two ends whatever its class, and at a junction only an end of the same class, link flag, direction and name within 30 degrees of straight on. A rule that needs "the same street" checks class and name itself.
- A repair must reach a fixed point: the engine reruns every rule after each rule's repairs, again after the provisional proposals, and the bake stops (REPAIR-01) when a second run on the result would still repair something. A rule whose auto fix is only a decision (`profile=smoothed`, `corridor=cut`) must skip features that carry that mark, and a shift must leave the stretch at both ends of an edge alone (`blendLength`) in the detect and in the applier alike.
- Provisional proposals change the graph too (53 joins, 2,400 widths on the pilot), so a second run lists a slightly different queue (edges cut by a join get ids like `w123.0/1`). Hero roads take no provisional change except their own spec (R-HERO-02).
- Pilot repair numbers (2026-11-11): 6,319 issues, 3,374 repaired on their own (53.4 percent), 2,845 queued (240 high impact), 100 explained. R-BLDG-01 is 4,916 of them: Overture's Google footprints stand within 1.2 m of the centre line of old city lanes, so half the building conflicts are over the 1.5 m bound or leave under 2 m of road. The census share of 73 percent came from OSM footprints only and a 5.5 m residential default.
- Foot ways and platforms are not in the graph: the foot over bridge rule gets them through `RuleEnv.walkways` (`repair/env.ts`); without them 51 of 55 bridge ends looked unconnected, with them 12. Stations are in `graph.features.stations` since P1-010.
- Overrides (P1-011, `tools/bake/repair/overrides.ts`): the engine applies an owner's patch after the first settle, when the graph is what it was while the queued issue was shown, and it takes the place of that issue's repair (status `overridden`, no provisional); a resolved patch no rule raises is applied anyway, a `disable_repair` patch only skips the repair. The second run that proves the fixed point must get `applied: true` (the patches are already in the graph) or `disable_repair` issues read as unconverged and REPAIR-01 fails. Stale = target gone by id and by anchor, or the applier refused; it counts in `health.json` and the file is never written by the bake. A patch outside the stage outline is `outsideStage`, not stale.
- `issues.json` x, z and previews are world metres (block metres plus the block's offset, 621.6 and 467.2 for the centre block); the graph, rules and repair stay in block metres. A patch anchor is lat and lon, so overrides survive a recentre.
- Override matching to an issue is by id, or by rule plus patch targets among the issue's features: edge ids change when an extract changes (`w123.0`), which is why the anchor fallback remaps ids inside the patch params too (`remapParams`).
- `purity.test.ts` also bans `node:fs` in `.test.ts` files under tools/bake/: a test that reads files is a `.test.mjs`.
- Nothing reads `harness/reports/map/` in a fresh clone or the verifier's snapshot (harness/reports is gitignored): tests that need a report make one with `bake()` into a temp folder and skip when `data/pilot_centre/` is missing too.
- Lane model (P1-012, `tools/bake/roads/network.ts`, split in P1-031 into `netzones.ts`, `netclusters.ts`, `netstrips.ts` with shared types in `netctx.ts`): `npm run bake -- pilot --stage lanes --offline` (model 2 to 7 s, 2 rounds; whole bake about 20 s). It reads the repaired graph and never moves a centre line. Pilot numbers 2026-11-12: 5,823 strips, 9,150 driving lanes (567 lane km, 1,094 gali lanes), 141 split zones, 2,827 junctions with 21,140 paths for 19,113 arm pairs, 0 continuity breaks, 917 explained ends.
- Lane counts must be conserved at a split zone or they run away: a zone carries main plus minor lanes, so a default count on the minor branch is lowered to what the road before can feed, a short road takes the zone's first section whole (`adopt`), and a count the model settled (`fixed`) never spreads along the street or across the median (it did: secondary carriageways reached 14 lanes). A raise is capped at one lane over the street's own count and at the room beside the paired carriageway (`EdgeLanes.cap`).
- `planSplit` names lanes by index when it runs: a road that is the branch of one zone and the road before another needs its gore side cut (`kerbOnly`) before the second zone is planned, so zones are planned twice (a first pass on a copy). Roundabout entries and splits within 18 m of a junction node are junctions, not zones.
- Junction paths: a fillet is as tight as its shorter side, so `turnPath` takes the wider of fillet and biarc. A cut asked for by a path (`minCutOf`) must also reach the J2 fallback of `planJunction`. Rule 3 (no turn over 135 degrees) must never close a road: an arm with no other way keeps its least sharp turn. 4 pilot pairs stay under 4 m (two roads side by side at a hairpin); they are listed in `health.json` `lanes.paths.tight`.
- Strips on each other in plan (P1-031 819 m2, P1-033 0.84 m2 in 3 pairs): five causes, five places. Forks the cluster rule builds as junctions and arms that leave side by side: `junction.ts clearCuts` cuts an arm past the other's carriageway (looks 400 m out: a loop road came back 250 m along its arm). Service loops drawn with right angles: `smoothLine` maxDev (`blockgraph.ts MAX_LINE_DEV_M` 0.5 m; it bowed 8 m). Lane model wider than the graph width (hero cross-section, zone, inferred lanes): R-LANE-06 from the lane feedback. Free ends into another road (dead ends, stage edge): `clip.ts`. A quad across a tunnel or bridge start: a station stands just past every level change (`pieceAt` gives the earlier piece at exactly `s1`). Classify leftovers with the scratch pattern: group `stripOverlaps` by strip pair, then ends (junction, joint, explained kind), shared node, angle.
- A repair rule whose shift can leave a residue (a fade at a fixed end) must mark the pair as done (`beside_with`, `gore_shift_m`) even when the applier moves nothing, and the detect must skip marked pairs, or REPAIR-01 fails with issues that "could still repair" (main.ts now lists them by rule). A detect must also skip what the applier cannot move (the nearest point is the other road's own end).
- A lane width under 2.4 m exists only as a gali lane (`LaneDef.tag === 'gali'`, never under 1.8 m): one shared lane on a residential, living street, service, pedestrian or unclassified edge whose width a tag or a building repair set. Bigger roads keep their class lane width when a building repair narrowed them (180 pilot edges).
- `pilot_lanes.test.mjs` runs the whole bake once (about 90 s since P1-033: the repair step builds the lane model up to three times): memoise the promise (`done ??= run()`) and give every test a long timeout, or the first test times out and the second starts a second bake.

- Lane feedback (P1-033, `repair/feedback.ts`): conflicts carry edge ids and their ways; R-LANE-06 finds an edge by id, else by way near the point (ids like `w123.1` exist only after a join or cut). `minSep` is absolute (the distance on the graph the lanes were built on plus the depth), so a run that starts from the raw graph still ends far enough apart.
- A queued issue can appear only after the provisional step (a branch moved closer, a width changed): the engine gives new queued issues their provisional too, up to 3 rounds (`PROVISIONAL_ROUNDS`); with one round a second run applied R-LANE-04 then repaired R-TERR-03 and REPAIR-01 failed.
- Elevation solver (P1-013, `tools/bake/elevation/`): `npm run bake -- pilot --stage elevation --offline` (whole bake about 110 s; the step solves twice for the same-twice check, 10 s each). It reads the lane model the lanes step made and writes `data/pilot_centre/elevation.json` (heights follow `lanes.json` strip for strip, station for station), `health.json` `elevation` and two pictures in `harness/reports/map/`. `roads/elevation.ts` is still the dense prototype that `site.ts` uses.
- A joint ties strip ends that can be 12 m apart sideways (a zone end and its branch): it means one height across that cross-section, not one point. Profiles therefore run along chains of strips by arc length, never by x and z.
- Least squares arches a free deck between far supports (ITO: 2.5 m above every clearance, held up by its two ramp crossings 240 m apart). A weak pull to the ground does not fix it without steepening the ramps; the ceiling of the second phase does. Any new "free from the ground" stretch needs the same thought (a dead end tunnel dived 25 m until tunnels got a pull to their cover depth).
- "Over a junction" is a point in polygon test on the ring of the arms' cut corners (`junctionRing`), not a radius: with a radius a flyover that starts beside a junction was asked to stand 7 m over it 12 m from its own start.
- A strip's grade limit must cover both knot intervals around its points (the spline blends them): painting only the intervals a strip overlaps let a 5 percent zone read 5.04 beside a 6 percent link. The station check in `checks.ts` found it; keep that check independent of the solver's knots.
- Map edge ties are stiff springs, not fixed heights: three ends (Bhairon Marg under a railway bridge 40 m inside the edge, Mori Gate Road) made grade and clearance impossible together when fixed. Every limit that has no cap must hold on a level road, or the multipliers never settle.
- OSM tags a tunnel ramp as layer -1 from its first node at an at grade junction, and passages through buildings as tunnel at layer 0: tags only order two roads where their surfaces overlap, and a layer 0 tunnel is a passage (`input.ts tunnelKinds`).
- The pilot tests bake side by side into the same `data/pilot_centre/` files: read a build product with a retry (`pilot_elevation.test.mjs readJson`), it may be half written.
- Road mesh (P1-014, `tools/bake/mesh/`): `npm run bake -- pilot --stage mesh --offline` (whole bake about 2 minutes; the mesh builds in 6 to 7 s, twice for the same-twice check, checks 3 s). It reads the lane build and the heights in memory (`mesh/input.ts`) and writes `health.json` `mesh`, four pictures `harness/reports/map/mesh_pilot_<site>.png` and the bench tiles `game/bench/roads/gen/<site>.ktl` (gitignored); then `node harness/bench/arch.mjs roads` takes the shots `harness/reports/arch/roads/roads_<site>_<pose>.png`.
- The top surface is plan faces over welded vertices (`faces.ts`), not triangles: watertight means every face edge has its twin, so a vertex of one face on another face's edge is put into that edge (`conform.ts`, 2 mm). A wider tolerance made it worse (edges used twice, 5 mm and 1 cm both tried). What the T joint repair cannot fix must be fixed where it comes from: joint rows that differ by millimetres are pinned onto each other (`Borders`, 452 unjoined ends without it), corners the polygon set made are snapped back onto existing vertices (4 mm).
- clipper2-ts results need three clean-ups before they are faces: holes (cut into slabs along lines that cross every hole; a hole kept as a face lay on the strip inside it), hairline necks between two neighbours that do not quite touch (drawn in and out by 1.5 mm), whiskers (a ring that runs out and straight back). Without them: edges used twice and one loop of 30,000 edges that never closed.
- A strip folds on the inside of a bend tighter than its width (the inner kerb quad comes out inside out): 2 pilot strips. Everything that asks where a strip is must use the unfolded, pinned borders (`Borders.of`), also the neighbour quads the junction builder subtracts: a raw bow-tie quad pokes into the junction and cut a triangle out of its ring.
- Kerb faces are not placed by rule: `solids.ts` draws a vertical face wherever two faces that share an edge differ in height. So the kerb height check (0.15 m) holds exactly beside planned faces, and a surface the polygon set made (heights spread from the arms' cut lines) shows steps of other heights beside kerbs: counted apart (`kerbs.beside`).
- Levels for overlap are sheets from the solved kinds, not the layer tags (`stripfaces.ts sheetOf`): ground (at grade, fill, cuts under 2.5 m), decks and lowered roads by tag. With tags alone a ramp on fill beside a road at grade is missed (13.4 m2 on the pilot, Kashmere Gate) and the tunnel mouth pair counts though it lies on two levels.
- Lane model defects the mesh shows and cannot fix: 63 junction clusters whose planned ring crosses itself (J2), 3 of them with cuts 200 to 320 m out (`fallback: the ring crosses itself`), whose lane paths wander over a square kilometre: their surface is the union of the lane path corridors (10 surfaces over 3,000 m2 in `health.json` `mesh.oversize`). Long `clearCuts` make junction rings that span other strips: the polygon set cuts those faces back (487 faces of 315 junctions).
- Bench tiles are a cut of the production mesh plus two stand-in things: lane lines as strips (T1 has no UV channel; the mesh carries the lane UV and border codes) and a ground sheet 0.3 m under the lowest road nearby with a verge down to it. Under a deck and over a tunnel the sheet stays at the ground model; in an open cut it must follow the road or the ray probe hits the sheet 1.2 m above the lane.
- Ground (P1-015, `tools/bake/terrain/`): `npm run bake -- pilot --stage terrain --offline` (whole bake about 2.5 minutes; one ground build 5 s, twice for the same-hash check, checks 2.5 s). It reads the road mesh, the repaired graph (footprints, water, open land, outline) and the ground model in memory and writes `health.json` `terrain`, pictures `harness/reports/map/ground_pilot_<site>.png` and the bench tiles again on the production ground (`game/bench/roads/gen/`, five sites with `yamuna`); then `node harness/bench/arch.mjs roads` takes the shots, and a pose with a `shot` name is also saved as `harness/reports/arch/roads/<shot>.png` (`ground_cp_eye`, `ground_ito_ramp_foot`, `ground_yamuna_bank`).
- The ground does not use the mesh's `cut` code as it is (`terrain/collect.ts meetOf`): every tunnel face is `CUT_NONE` in the mesh, also a tunnel ramp open to the sky, and a junction with one deck or tunnel arm is `CUT_NONE` although it lies on the ground level sheet. Taken as they are, the ground lay like a lid on tunnel ramps and ran through 130 junction surfaces.
- Two road faces of different heights at one vertex (a footpath end beside a carriageway end, a kerb beside a wall) cannot share one ground height: the ground has corner heights (`surface.ts`), and a triangle that fills the corner between two such edges gets a point inside first (`triangulate.ts`, 1,403 on the pilot). Without it 1,160 corners kept a 0.15 m gap.
- Checks that walk a road edge must follow the triangulation's own pieces of it (`RoadEdges`), not "the nearest vertex within 4 mm": two crossings 2 mm apart read as a 0.15 m gap, and a point test with a 3 mm margin called 130 sample points on kerb faces "not road".
- A ray straight down the edge two triangles share can pass between them when they belong to two collider chunks (two bodies): one cp probe on a station line missed (11 of 12) once the ground changed the chunks, and hit again 2 mm to any side. Bench probes now stand 5 cm past the station line (`meshout.ts`). `colliderChunks` also leaves out triangles under a square millimetre (no area as 32-bit floats).
- The pilot has a road 13 m under the ground model (around world 1086, -4440, between a cut and a tunnel): the ground ends at the top of its walls there. Heights like that come from the elevation solve, not from the ground.
- Production tiles (P1-016, folder moved in P1-017): `npm run bake -- pilot --offline` writes `game/world/tiles/` (gitignored; the stage's `tiles` field in districts.json) (417 tiles, 358 MB, the step 14 s on top of the 2.5 minute bake); `game/tests/test_tiles.gd` reads the largest one when it is there. The writer snaps every vertex to 1/1024 m in world metres, so stored numbers are exact float32 and seams are the same point in both tiles; the snap turns over about 2,200 needle triangles (left out, `flipped`). Never compare tile vertices with a tolerance smaller than the snap against unsnapped mesh numbers.
- Tile magnitude margin is 250 m: shells go whole to their centroid's tile and reach up to about 180 m past it (Red Fort walls, station roofs and Bharat Mandapam are 300 to 610 m long: over 200 m past they are cut per tile), a road triangle up to about 140 m. Junction paths can be over 1 km long (the widest junctions): lanes and paths are both cut at the tile lines (`tile/lanes.ts`).
- Ray checks on tiles (`tile/checks.ts`) count front faces looking up only, as Godot's concave shapes do (front normal -(b - a) x (c - a)); a check of a tile must load its 8 neighbours too, because a road triangle of the next tile covers a corner (the ground there is cut away).

It overwrites `harness/reports/map/health.json` with a partial report (no pictures; `timing.json` beside it says `partial: true`): run a whole bake before reading the report or showing the dashboard.
