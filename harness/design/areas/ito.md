- **Changed since the old photos:** Pragati Maidan area: the Pragati Maidan Integrated Transit Corridor and its tunnel opened in June 2022; the ITPO complex (Bharat Mandapam) was rebuilt and opened 2023. Photos before 2022 are marked OLD.
- **Changed since the old photos:** Photos of the ITO crossing and the Pragati Maidan tunnel from road level were not found among the free dated files; see Photo requests.
- **Facade colours (sampled):** k-means on the building band (18% to 52% of the frame height, sky, foliage, black and shadow removed, shown dark to light) of 8 street-level current photos (night shots skipped): #665B55 (22%), #D56E49 (12%), #94837F (11%), #D2A696 (7%), #DED6D1 (7%). - Trees: avenue trees along Barakhamba Road; ring road edges are mostly bare with some planted strips (seen).
- Signs: green street signs, blue directional boards, the ITO metro station entrance with its yellow and blue shop boards, CNG bus route boards (seen).

## Footpaths

Wide pavements for an arterial, about 3 to 4 m, grey or red concrete paver, broken in places, with a foot-over skywalk of 535 m linking ITO and Pragati Maidan metro (see sources). Kerbs painted yellow and black. Pedestrians often walk in the road.

## Road surface and markings

Six to eight lanes at the crossing, asphalt of mixed age, thick white lane lines, stop lines and zebra crossings often faded. Flyovers carry Vikas Marg traffic over the junction; ramps with concrete jersey barriers, yellow and black kerb marks. Surface patched, oil-stained (heavy traffic).

## Street lamps

Tall highmast and curved-arm steel poles on the flyovers and the main road, sodium orange at night. Smaller double-arm poles on the side streets.

## Boundaries

Metal railing on the flyover, concrete parapets, jersey barriers, brick boundary walls of old bungalow-era blocks (Todarmal Lane, brick walls with green NDMC signs). Police barricades at the crossing.

## Buildings

Government and media offices: Delhi Police Headquarters glass and concrete slab, about 10 to 15 floors, Vikas Minar, Doordarshan Bhawan, newspaper offices on Bahadur Shah Zafar Marg, Delhi High Court and Supreme Court area. Mixed 4 to 12 floor slab blocks, grey and brown concrete, tinted glass, AC boxes. Behind it the Purana Quila walls rise above trees. Metro stations are standard.

## Trees and planting

Large roadside trees (neem, peepal, banyan) along Safdar Hashmi Marg and Tilak Marg; thin planting on the flyover verges; bushes in the median.

## Vendors and street life

Heavy but anonymous traffic: office workers, journalists, tea stalls, juice carts, bus stops with crowds, auto-rickshaw queues. Fewer tourists. Night: tea stalls and snack carts near the metro.

## Parking and vehicles

Buses (DTC green), autos, taxis, delivery vans, cars. Informal roadside parking all the way along the dividers. Cycle and cart lanes are missing.

## Typical clutter and wear

Wear 0.4. Dust, soot on the underside of the flyover, bill posters, torn flex banners, hoardings, mud. Concrete grey-brown, rarely painted.

## What makes this area unlike the others

The only pilot area built around flyovers and a huge signal-free junction, with grey slab offices and the skywalk. The sense is infrastructure scale and noise, not heritage. Closest thing to modern Delhi traffic.

## Edge blending (what changes first)

Edges: to the west the Mandi House and Bengali Market side shifts to red-brick colonial-era blocks and cultural buildings; to the east the Yamuna bridge and Pragati Maidan with Purana Quila. First change: building age and colour (brick instead of concrete), lamps swap from highmast to double-arm.

## Street-level top-up (P2-002, 2026-11-13)

Where this section differs from an older line above, this section wins. Infrastructure variants (flyovers, metro, bridges, subways, skywalks) are in `harness/design/reference/infrastructure/variant_map.md`; this sheet does not repeat them. Tile numbers are board tiles.

**Footpaths.** Bhairon Marg (42 to 44): about 2.5 to 3 m of red-brown interlocking paver, a yellow tactile guide strip along the road edge, a black and white painted kerb at the bus bay, a planted verge 1 to 2 m wide between walk and road. Safdar Hashmi Marg (33 to 37): a raised kerb walk about 2 to 2.5 m wide in grey paver, half finished in places (bare earth strip, stacked kerb stones). Todarmal Lane (38, 39): almost no walk on the wall side, a strip about 1 m wide of broken concrete along a brick wall.

**Road surface and markings.** Jawaharlal Nehru Marg and Bhairon Marg (42, 43, 46, 47): three lanes each way, each carriageway about 10 to 11 m, grey asphalt in fair state, white dashed lane lines, white turn arrows, a stop line and zebra bars at junction mouths (34 to 36, bars about 0.5 m wide, crossing about 3 m deep). Medians are 2 to 3 m, kerbed, painted black and white, planted with shrubs and small trees. A left-turn slip lane with a triangle island at the Jawaharlal Nehru Marg junction (46, 47).

**Street lamps.** Tall steel poles in the median with two curved arms, about 10 to 12 m high, spaced about 35 to 45 m (43, 46, 47). Single bracket poles on the metro side (25). Lit advertising panels at bus shelters (44). Heavy overhead cables along the side streets.

**Boundaries.** Red-brown brick wall about 2 m high with stone coping and iron gate leaves (38, 39). Yellow Delhi Police barricades across the road mouths (34, 36, 37). Dark iron ornamental gate at the Shaheedi Park side (30). A yellow and black silence-zone sign on the wall (38).

**Buildings.** Government slabs from the 1970s to 2010s in leafy compounds: Shakti Sadan (26), a concrete slab about 10 storeys with blue horizontal bands and deep window recesses; PWD and Police headquarters (28, 29), 12 to 15 storey grid fronts in grey concrete and glass; Doordarshan Bhawan (41), an orange-brown brick block of about 8 storeys. The elevated metro track and masts run along the Ring Road edge (25).

**Trees and planting.** Dense mature canopy of neem, rain tree and peepal, about 12 to 18 m high, over Safdar Hashmi Marg and Todarmal Lane (33 to 40). Flowering gulmohar and median shrubs along Bhairon Marg (42, 43).

**Vendors and street life.** Students and office staff cross on foot at the Mandi House mouth (34 to 37), with e-rickshaws and autos waiting, a tea stall and a handcart at the corner, a dog asleep on the verge (38, 39). The Mandi House metro concourse (31, 32) has a granite floor with black stripe bands and a painted station board.

**Parking and vehicles.** Autos in green and yellow and e-rickshaws at the mouths, white cabs, buses on the ring roads, police cars at the barricade (34). Convoys on the flyover approach in three lanes (27). Parking is mostly informal at the kerb.

**Typical clutter and wear.** Green street-name boards in three scripts on single posts (33, 38, 40), electrical junction boxes, bus shelters with large ad panels (44), signal poles, a pedestrian subway with tiled floor and a mural wall (45). Wear on the side streets about 0.5; main roads about 0.35. Heavy traffic soils the walls and kerbs.

**What sets it apart (added).** Wide tree-lined avenues of grey government slabs, with a kerbed median and a painted black and white edge, and a very busy crossing under flyovers, metro and skywalk. Mandi House (culture and press) is a few hundred metres west.

## Map notes (P2-002)

Read from the photos; each item is a check against OSM and satellite, not a final blueprint.
- Medians on Jawaharlal Nehru Marg and Bhairon Marg are kerbed and 2 to 3 m wide (42, 43, 46, 47). Check OSM has these as separate carriageways, with the width.
- Carriageway width about 10 to 11 m per direction (3 lanes), plus a slip lane with a triangle island at the Jawaharlal Nehru Marg junction (46, 47). Check for a separate slip way.
