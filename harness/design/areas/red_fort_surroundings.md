### What it is made of today

- **Changed since the old photos:** No rebuild of the fort forecourt known since 2019; barricades and CISF posts change around 15 August. Photos from 2019 on count. Old photos (1966 and earlier) are marked OLD.
- **Facade colours (sampled):** k-means on the building band (18% to 52% of the frame height, sky, foliage, black and shadow removed, shown dark to light) of 9 street-level current photos (night shots skipped): #7A5C54 (25%), #A37361 (19%), #A99384 (7%), #C0C0C3 (22%), #D1CFD1 (9%). Treat as the range; pick facade paints from these, and bright colours only where a photo shows them.
- **Road surface sampled:** the bottom centre of the same photos, largest colour cluster #B2987F, others #80624E, #413129 (cars, paint and shadow are mixed in, so read the main value as the surface and judge it against the photos).
- Road surface and colour: wide dark grey asphalt carriageway with white lane lines along the fort wall, beside it a wide forecourt of grey and red-pink stone flags (seen, 2025 wall series).
- Kerbs: stone kerb at the forecourt edge, yellow and orange barricades and CISF posts across it (seen).
- Footpath: broad red-pink sandstone and grey stone flagged forecourt at the Lahori Gate; grass strips along the dry moat (seen).
- Median: none seen along the fort wall road; at the Chandni Chowk end a blue P parking board and a security cabin sit at the road edge (seen, C-photo Lal Qila from Chandni Chowk, 2024).
- Poles and wires: black ornamental lamp posts along the wall, tall mast lights; very few overhead wires on the fort side, many on the Chandni Chowk side (seen).
- Lights: the wall is floodlit in warm orange-yellow at night (seen, 2019 and 2023 night shots).
- Trees: a few large trees at the wall foot, bare moat (seen).
- Signs: parking board with a blue P, ASI and CISF boards, brown tourist boards (seen).
- Typical clutter: crowds in the forecourt, autos and e-rickshaws at the edge, police vans, flag poles (seen).

## Footpaths

On Netaji Subhash Marg: about 2.5 m of broken concrete or grey tile footpath beside a low retaining wall. At the Lahori Gate plaza: a wide open asphalt and stone forecourt with painted crowd lines.

## Road surface and markings

Wide arterial, 3 lanes each way, with a kerbed central divider and clear dashed lines. Grey and worn asphalt, patched. At the fort: open asphalt plaza with painted zones, no markings, yellow police barricades.

## Street lamps

Tall steel arm poles on the road, 9 to 10 m, single curved arms. Around the fort: modern floodlights at ground level lighting the red sandstone from below.

## Boundaries

The fort wall itself, 18 to 33 m high red sandstone with a dry moat; low railing along the road; police barricades; iron railing along the retaining wall. Security checkpoints at the gate.

## Buildings

Red Fort: red Agra sandstone walls with battlements, domed chhatris, the Lahori Gate with the flag and white marble top. Across the road: plain white 3 to 4 storey police and administrative blocks and Old Delhi shops. Colours: red sandstone #a4533b, white marble.

## Trees and planting

Trees on the road side (neem, gulmohar), clipped bushes, the green moat lawn between wall and road. A few big trees at the Subhash Park. Lawn is short grass.

## Vendors and street life

Tourists in lines at the gate, rickshaw pullers, vendors selling flags and snacks, ticket queues, guards. Independence Day crowds on 15 August. Pigeons.

## Parking and vehicles

Tour buses and autos in lines on the plaza, rickshaws waiting, police vans at the barricades. Not much private parking.

## Typical clutter and wear

Wear 0.35. Stone is dusty and stained, bird droppings, litter near the entrance. Plenty of barricades and signage boards in Hindi and English.

## What makes this area unlike the others

Monumental red wall as the backdrop to a street: a continuous 20 to 30 m red sandstone rampart with a single huge gate. No other area has red sandstone or a security-managed plaza.

## Edge blending (what changes first)

Edges: Lahori Gate plaza opens west to Chandni Chowk (the bazaar starts after about 80 m, wires and shop signs first); north to Netaji Subhash Marg and Subhash Park; the ring road to the east. First change: boards and wires appear, the white blocks give way to red-brown shops.

## Street-level top-up (P2-002, 2026-11-13)

Where this section differs from an older line above, this section wins. Tile numbers are board tiles.

**Footpaths.** The area in front of the Lahori Gate is a plaza, not a road with walks: black asphalt and stone, about 40 to 60 m across, with orange traffic cones marking the visitor queue (32, 33). Along the moat a kerb with painted orange and white. The Lal Quila metro entrance is a stepped plaza of polished stone with a glass canopy (36). On the Chandni Chowk Road side footpaths are narrow, about 1.5 to 2.5 m, shared with hawkers (35, 41, 44).

**Road surface and markings.** Netaji Subhash Marg at the Lahori Gate opens to a very wide asphalt space with few markings (28, 29, 33). Worn black asphalt with dust and some painted bars at the junction. Chandni Chowk Road has wider lanes with lane lines and a median (41).

**Street lamps.** Tall twin-arm poles about 10 to 12 m at the junction and the plaza (28, 29, 32). Ground floodlights along the wall (see sheet above). Single curved-arm poles on the Chandni Chowk side (41).

**Boundaries.** The dry moat about 15 to 20 m wide with grass banks and a 1 m red stone parapet (34). The rampart batter wall in red Agra sandstone rises from the moat (33, 34, 37, 38). Yellow barricades across vendor and visitor lines (35). Iron railing around the temple compounds (43).

**Buildings.** The Lahori Gate bastions and the Chatta Chowk covered arcade in red sandstone (25 to 31). Across the road: temples in red and white (the Jain temple cluster, 40, 46), the Gauri Shankar temple (43), Fatehpuri mosque courtyard (42), old shops with painted signs (41, 44). A fast-food frontage and heritage block at the Chandni Chowk crossing, only as a type note: do not use real brands.

**Trees and planting.** Bare babul and neem trees in the parking lot and along the moat (37, 38); a large tree on the central island of the junction (28, 29).

**Vendors and street life.** Lemonade and snack sellers at the barricades (35), visitors with cameras in loose groups and queues (32), rickshaw pullers waiting at the road (45), shopkeepers on the Chandni Chowk side (44). People of all ages and dress.

**Parking and vehicles.** Rows of cars, tourist buses and autos in the lot (37, 38), cycle rickshaws, e-rickshaws and autos on the road (41, 45).

**Typical clutter and wear.** Wear about 0.35. Orange cones, queue barriers, signs, overhead wires on the temple side, dust on the stone.

**What sets it apart (added).** A very large open plaza in front of a red sandstone wall, with the junction opening onto it, and a dry moat on one side.

## Map notes (P2-002)

- The Lahori Gate approach is an open plaza about 40 to 60 m across (28, 29, 32, 33). Check OSM: it likely shows roads and parking where the photos show open paving. Model as a plaza polygon with a few service lanes.
- A small central island with a traffic police booth, a big tree and a tall lamp at the Netaji Subhash Marg and Chandni Chowk Road junction (28, 29).
- Dry moat about 15 to 20 m wide on the fort side with a 1 m parapet (34). Check it is not modelled as road.
