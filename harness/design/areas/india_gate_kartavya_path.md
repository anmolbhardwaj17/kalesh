## Footpaths

Each side of the carriageway has a wide band of reddish-pink crushed stone and paved walk about 6 to 8 m, then lawn. A paved central line of tiled path at the central vista, with a stone edge. Sandstone-pink colour. Pedestrians walk on the road in the evening after closure. Kerb is plain stone, very low.

## Road surface and markings

Three lanes each side, grey asphalt in very good condition, a yellow solid centre line (not dashed) and white dashed lane lines, large open junctions with the cross roads. No kerb paint stripes. Very clean, no potholes. Pale marks at the pedestrian crossing.

## Street lamps

Classical cast lamps: black posts about 6 to 8 m tall with a lantern head, regular 30 m spacing along both sides. Warm light. India Gate is lit by floodlights.

## Boundaries

Low green iron railing with white stone posts and chains along the lawn edge and the canal edge. Low chain-link bollards at the hexagon. Police barricades stripe in yellow and black. No walls.

## Buildings

India Gate: a 42 m war memorial arch, buff Bharatpur sandstone. North Block and South Block at Raisina: red and cream sandstone, domed chhatris, long horizontal colonnades. Everything is low-rise and monumental; skyline is flat apart from the domes. No shop frontage, no signs.

## Trees and planting

Rows of large avenue trees (jamun, neem, arjun) lining both sides and forming a visual wall; big open green lawns; a sunken canal with boat rides. The hexagon around India Gate is a mown lawn with tree rows at its edges.

## Vendors and street life

Evening crowds: families, ice cream and balloon vendors on the lawns, flags and souvenirs (the vendors sit outside the lawns near the gate). Joggers and cyclists in the early morning. Parade rehearsal and Republic Day events.

## Parking and vehicles

Very few parked cars. Buses and cars pass through; tourist buses park at the India Gate hexagon edge; police vans at barricades.

## Typical clutter and wear

Wear 0.0 to 0.03 (Lutyens zone). Very clean; the odd leaf litter, dust on stone.

## What makes this area unlike the others

The widest, flattest, most formal space in the game: a straight central vista, a monumental arch at one end, sandstone ministries at the other, lawns and canals on both sides. No shops, no wires, no hoardings. If it looks busy, it is wrong.

## Edge blending (what changes first)

Edges: the cross avenues (Janpath, Man Singh Road, Shahjahan Road) bring in low walls and bungalow roofs. The vista holds right up to the hexagon, then turns to avenue trees and then to a mixed zone near ITO to the east. First change: the lamp spacing and the lawn width drop; walls and gates replace lawns.
