### What it is made of today

- **Changed since the old photos:** No big rebuild since 2019 apart from repaving and metro works at the edges. The 2020 and 2021 lockdown photos show the empty circle and the colonnade clearly and are kept as current (the buildings and paving did not change); crowds and vehicles come from the 2019 and 2022 photos.
- **Facade colours (sampled):** k-means on the building band (18% to 52% of the frame height, sky, foliage, black and shadow removed, shown dark to light) of 13 street-level current photos (night shots skipped): #6C5439 (15%), #716A5F (22%), #6F83A0 (7%), #A68D73 (15%), #CFC0AB (16%). Treat as the range; pick facade paints from these, and bright colours only where a photo shows them.
- **Road surface sampled:** the bottom centre of the same photos, largest colour cluster #6D6151, others #383628, #A8A29A (cars, paint and shadow are mixed in, so read the main value as the surface and judge it against the photos).
- Road surface and colour: smooth dark grey asphalt, wide inner-circle carriageway, white lane and edge lines; at night near-black with orange sodium glare (seen, 2020 empty circle, 2019 night shot).
- Kerbs: stone kerb with a black and yellow painted stripe at the circle edge and at refuges (seen, 2019 night shot).
- Footpath: inside the colonnade dark grey stone flags with pale grey joint bands, about 4 to 5 m wide; outside the arcade a grey cement paver strip with a yellow tactile guide strip (seen, C-photos of the colonnade 2020 and 2021).
- Median and edges: a planter strip at the kerb with white and pink flowering shrubs (bougainvillea, petunia); dark green iron railing, about 1 m, along the central park (seen).
- Poles and wires: tall steel poles with cobra-head lamps; the arcade has hanging tube lights under the ceiling; few overhead wires (seen).
- Lights: sodium orange street lights and cool white on the facades at night; the corner blocks are lit pale yellow-cream (seen, 2019 night shot).
- Trees: large rain trees and neem along the circle, canopy over the footpath, palms in the park (seen).
- Signs: white stucco facades carry bar and restaurant boards in red and white, blue bank boards at the upper arcade, block letters on the white faces, a dark granite plaque at State Entry Road, green NDMC street signs (seen).
- Typical clutter: green and yellow autos, vendors with balloons in the arcade, pigeons, parked cars on the outer edge, metro entrance (seen, 2021 and 2022 shots).
- Facade: white and cream lime stucco, Tuscan columns about 0.7 m diameter, round arches, a cornice, upper storey with white balustrade and arched windows (seen).

## Footpaths

Colonnade side: about 6 m of pale stone paving (cream and grey slabs, some replaced in a 2010 to 2013 renovation) running under and in front of the columns. Dead flat, no steps at the column line. Cast bollards at the kerb. Kerb about 15 cm, grey stone. Central Park side: about 3 m, a flower bed between kerb and footpath, a tactile strip down the middle. Condition: clean in front of big brands, patched and torn up where works are on.

## Road surface and markings

Four drive lanes of dark asphalt, white dashed lane lines, solid white edge line on the park side. Painted kerb stripes in yellow and black at junction mouths and block corners. Junction surface with each radial is one open asphalt area. Condition: smooth, repainted often; wet reflections at night.

## Street lamps

Steel poles with two arms, about 9 m, spaced about 28 m on the planter edge, warm white to sodium light (hero road spec). Short lantern-style lamps appear only on the older arches (Connaught High Street arch, a lantern on a bracket beside a clock). At festivals the colonnade is wrapped in warm fairy lights.

## Boundaries

Green steel railing on a low plinth at the back of the Central Park footpath, about 1.2 m. Clipped hedge and kerbed planter between the carriageway and the parking bay. Cast bollards on the colonnade footpath. No walls, no gates. Police barricades appear on event days.

## Buildings

Two storey colonial classical blocks (A to N), white or cream lime render, Tuscan columns on the ground floor, a continuous parapet, arched pavilions at block ends and at gaps. Each block is about 11.5 m high and one continuous curved front. Windows above the columns are tall, shuttered, often with window ACs. Shop signage is brand boards hung on the column heads and across the first floor. Behind the blocks, glass office towers of 15 to 25 floors show on the skyline.

## Trees and planting

Large deciduous street trees in pits in the paving of the colonnade footpath, uneven spacing, about 12 m high. Central Park is open lawn with a few big trees, rose and flower beds, and the giant tricolour flagpole. Hibiscus and bougainvillea in the planters.

## Vendors and street life

Day: office workers, shoppers, tourists, a few hawkers (pirated books, maps, small toys) on the footpath edge. Night: dense crowds under the colonnade, food and cart sellers at the kerb, auto-rickshaws queuing. Calm and upper-middle-class compared with Janpath or Chandni Chowk.

## Parking and vehicles

Angled car parking between the footpath and the planter, SUVs and sedans, a few auto-rickshaws (green and yellow), two-wheelers parked tight at the column line, a scooter and cycle beside a pole in rain. DTC and cluster buses on the radial approaches.

## Typical clutter and wear

Wear 0.15. Clean render, but AC drip stains, wires across the arches, tarps where renovation scaffolding stands. Litter is low. Pigeons on parapets.

## What makes this area unlike the others

Perfect circle of white colonnaded blocks, one continuous classical order, with a lawned park in the middle and a huge flag. Everything is pale, even-toned and symmetrical. No other pilot area has columns, a ring road and a central park together. The most formal and tidy of the commercial areas, but still crowded at night.

## Edge blending (what changes first)

Moving outward across the outer circle and radials the first change is the colonnade: it becomes a smaller arcade and then shopfronts; footpaths shrink from 6 m to 3 m; lamps stay the same two-arm steel pole. Buildings stay cream for one block, then turn to mixed 4 to 8 storey offices.
