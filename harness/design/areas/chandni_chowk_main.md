### What it is made of today

- **Changed since the old photos:** Chandni Chowk main road was redeveloped (about 2021): red sandstone paving across the street, no central median, no railings, bollards, wider pedestrian space, hawkers moved, motor vehicles only at set hours (the access model already makes it car-free by day). Press and the 2021 map board (C-photos do not show the whole street). The 2008 median with iron railings, sodium double-arm poles and tram-era asphalt are gone.
- **Changed since the old photos:** Every photo older than 2022 is marked OLD: it shows the street before the redevelopment (2008 view, 2005 Vellut, 2014, 2015). Use the old ones only for what did not change: the shape of the bazaar blocks.
- **Facade colours (sampled):** k-means on the building band (18% to 52% of the frame height, sky, foliage, black and shadow removed, shown dark to light) of 13 street-level current photos (night shots skipped): #674B3C (11%), #70665F (13%), #9A7B64 (13%), #AD9D8D (18%), #CECAC5 (30%). Treat as the range; pick facade paints from these, and bright colours only where a photo shows them.
- **Road surface sampled:** the bottom centre of the same photos, largest colour cluster #271F1C, others #745D4E, #B39E8E (cars, paint and shadow are mixed in, so read the main value as the surface and judge it against the photos).
- Road surface and colour: the redevelopment is reported to use red sandstone paving (known). SEEN: pink-red sandstone slabs on the Fatehpuri Masjid forecourt (2024), but the ground at the Sis Ganj junction in the 2025 shots reads dark grey (asphalt or dark stone setts, not red). The carriageway down the middle is not clearly visible in any dated free photo: do not assume red there until a photo shows it (see Photo requests).
- Kerbs: none on the central section (flush paving, bollards at the ends, per redevelopment, known). Side lanes keep low concrete kerbs.
- Footpath: the whole street is footpath: sandstone slabs, stalls moved back, planters (known). Sis Ganj plaza steps and forecourt are sandstone too (seen).
- Median: none (removed in the redevelopment, known).
- Poles and wires: new street poles at the edges (known); overhead wires and signal cables still hang across the cross lanes and at the gurudwara end (seen in the 2025 Sis Ganj street shots).
- Lights: lamp posts at the edges plus shop lights; evening shots show neon and strip lights, garlands of fairy lights (seen, 2023 night view).
- Trees: a few large trees at the Fatehpuri end and in the Sis Ganj forecourt, potted plants in the gate arch (seen).
- Signs: SBI building has a blue and white bank board over the arcade; shop fascia boards in Hindi and English, blue, white and yellow, stacked three high, with vertical lit boards; the new ornamental gateway at the gurudwara end is peach-cream with maroon edging and carved white and pink jaali arches (seen, 2025). Facades at the Sis Ganj junction are peach, cream, white and maroon-brown plaster, three to four storeys, with lit hoardings (seen).
- Typical clutter: cycle rickshaws with red canopies, e-rickshaws, handcarts, crowds, umbrellas (seen, 2025 Sis Ganj shots).

## Footpaths

Before redevelopment (2008): narrow, broken footpaths about 1 to 1.5 m, fully taken over by stalls. After the redevelopment (about 2021): a wide pedestrian priority avenue with red sandstone paving and no kerbs on the central section. The game uses the current (post 2021) state, matching the access model (car-free by day): red sandstone paving, no median, bollards at the ends. Old photos are for the facades and the shops only. (Anmol 2026-11-14, decisions.md.)

## Road surface and markings

A busy central road: asphalt worn smooth, no lane lines in places, rickshaws and autos driving anywhere. Tram lines gone. Central median with iron railings . Surface oil-stained and patched.

## Street lamps

Tall double-arm sodium poles along the median at about 9 m. Shops light the street at night with bare bulbs and neon. Lanterns and garlands near the temples.

## Boundaries

Iron median railing, shutters, closed roll-down shutters in the morning. Gurudwara Sisganj gate with a gold dome, Fatehpuri Masjid at the west end. Metal barriers on the pedestrian side.

## Buildings

Facade: continuous 3 to 5 storey bazaar blocks, 19th and early 20th century, lime plaster and brick, arched windows, cantilever balconies, hoardings (DCB bank, ads on the top floor). Temples and the gurudwara with domes break the line. Many facades are painted in faded cream, pink, green, and blue (a teal mosque front). Wires hang across the street.

## Trees and planting

Few trees; a couple of large trees at the ends. Planters on the pedestrian section. Bougainvillea on balconies.

## Vendors and street life

The densest crowd in the pilot: rickshaws, hawkers, porters, shoppers, food stalls, flower garland sellers, eggs and snack sellers. Morning: closed shutters and sleeping porters.

## Parking and vehicles

Cycle rickshaws, e-rickshaws, autos, handcarts, scooters. No parking, vehicles stop anywhere.

## Typical clutter and wear

Wear 0.9. Grime, stained plaster, tangled wires, torn posters, garlands, dust and haze, litter, damp, signboard clusters.

## What makes this area unlike the others

The main axis of Old Delhi: a straight avenue from the Red Fort to Fatehpuri Masjid lined with mixed domes (temple, gurudwara, mosque), densely signed shop blocks and heavy wires. It is the one road that reads as a long straight gallery of faith and trade.

## Edge blending (what changes first)

Edges: east end hits the Red Fort and its open lawns; west end hits Fatehpuri Masjid and Khari Baoli; north and south side lanes (galis) become narrower first (road width falls from 15 m to 3 m), then building height rises and balconies join overhead.
