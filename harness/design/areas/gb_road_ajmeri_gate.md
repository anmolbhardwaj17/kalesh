# Area sheet: GB Road and Ajmeri Gate (Garstin Bastion Road, hardware street)

Status: draft from street-level reference research, P2-002 (2026-11-13). Id `gb_road_ajmeri_gate`. Internal reference only, never shipped. Board: `harness/design/areas/gb_road_ajmeri_gate_board.jpg`.

**Respect line (GAME.md "Respect rule", owner decision "GB Road treatment").** The street reads as a hardware and parts street: hardware shops at street level, rolled shutters, signboards, handcarts and tempos loading pipes and sheets. The red-light trade exists in the story only as background above the shops and is never drawn, photographed or used for reference here. No source below shows the trade or any person who lives or works upstairs, and none was searched for. People shown in the game on this street are shopkeepers, porters, riders, passers-by and families, with the same dignity and the same craft as in any other area. No sex, no nudity, no paying for sex as a mechanic, no exploitation of real people, no jokes at the expense of anyone who lives or works here.

**Evidence note.** 30 street-level photos (tiles 1 to 30), all Wikimedia Commons, openly viewable, each with link and licence in the table. Mapillary and Flickr need a login or token, so they were not used. Commons has no usable street view of the length of GB Road itself, so the sheet is built from the places at its ends and the streets of the same type: the Ajmeri Gate and the road along it (1 to 9), the station front at night and the rail line beside it (10, 11), the New Delhi station forecourt on the Ajmeri Gate side (12 to 22), Hauz Qazi Chowk (23 to 27), and the Delhi Gate and Turkman Gate on the old wall line (28 to 30). **Confidence: medium** for the Ajmeri Gate end, the gate, the hardware-shop building type and the station forecourt; **low** for the middle of GB Road, which has no openly licensed street-level photo. It cannot be higher until the owner adds his own photos, or Mapillary is used with a token. A line that says "about" was read from the photos by scale cues (a car is about 1.7 m wide, a person about 1.7 m tall) and is not surveyed.

## Footpaths

Mostly none. Shops reach the kerb and spill about 1 to 1.5 m onto broken concrete, with steps, stools and stacked stock (5 to 9). At the Ajmeri Gate the gate stands on a fenced island with the road passing on both sides (1 to 4). At the station forecourt a paved apron about 8 to 10 m wide, bounded by a yellow and black painted kerb (15, 21, 22).

## Road surface and markings

Ajmeri Gate Road: two lanes each way, about 10 to 12 m kerb to kerb at the gate, patched grey asphalt, parked autos and tempos on both edges, few readable markings (1, 5 to 7). The station forecourt is a wide asphalt apron with painted bays for autos and cabs in rows four to six wide (16, 17, 21). A speed hump or two near the gates (9, 18). GB Road itself, by general knowledge, runs about a kilometre; its width must be read from the OSM lanes.

## Street lamps

Tall poles with curved arms at the gate (1), a high mast with a cluster of heads over the forecourt (16, 17, 18), shorter curved-arm poles along the station front (21, 22). On the narrow stretch, shop tubes and bulbs light the pavement; many signs are lit.

## Boundaries

Iron railing around the gate island (1 to 4), yellow Delhi Police barricades with white stripes at the junction mouths and the gate (4, 24, 26), a school gate with an arched sign over an iron grille (8), rolled shutters in grey and blue on every ground floor (5 to 9), a kiosk-sized police booth at the Hauz Qazi junction (24 to 27). The station forecourt has a low kerbed edge, not a wall.

## Buildings

Hardware and parts shops at street level, with painted boards (metals, traders, hardware stores, pipes) in Hindi and English (5, 6, 7). Above, three to five storeys of shuttered upper floors: green-painted timber balconies and jharokhas (5), red brick and plaster with exposed cable bundles (7), curved-corner cream and yellow buildings with sunshades (9, 23 to 27), old colonial frontages (23 to 27). The Ajmeri Gate itself: red and grey stone, about 10 m tall, one deep pointed arch and a crenellated parapet, round bastions at the corners (1 to 4). The station on the Ajmeri Gate side: a long two-storey colonnade with white columns, red brick base and a painted wrap along the upper floor (12 to 15, 19), plus an orange multi-storey block with coloured panels on the Paharganj side (20). Rooftop water tanks, AC units and signage on the roofs.

## Trees and planting

Few. A neem or peepal above the old buildings or at the school (6, 8), a row of trees along the wall line at Delhi Gate (28 to 30). Almost none along the shops.

## Vendors and street life

A working street: porters, handcart (thela) and tempo loading, shopkeepers on stools at their thresholds, tea sellers, riders waiting in autos and e-rickshaws, cycle-rickshaw pullers, families and travellers heading for the station (1 to 3, 12 to 17). At the station forecourt, autos line up in rows with cabs and rickshaws waiting for fares (16, 17). Shops open in the morning and close early evening; shutters stay down upstairs.

## Parking and vehicles

Autos in green and yellow, e-rickshaws, cabs, cycle rickshaws, tempos and mini trucks, handcarts, motorbikes. The forecourt holds hundreds of autos at peak (16, 17).

## Typical clutter and wear

Wear about 0.7 to 0.8. Tangled overhead wires and cable bundles on the building fronts (5 to 7, 23 to 27), hoardings and shop boards, rooftop tanks, barricades, litter in the gutter, dust on the stone. The station forecourt is cleaner (0.4), with bright hoardings and a wrapped facade.

## What makes this area unlike the others

A hardware and auto-parts street at the foot of a 17th century red stone gate, with the old station colonnade and a sea of yellow autos on one side. The buildings are long runs of shuttered four-storey fronts with hand-painted trade boards, and the street is mostly about loading and moving goods.

## Edge blending (what changes first)

East: the old city wall line and the Hauz Qazi Chowk junction lead to Chawri Bazar and the Chandni Chowk lanes; hardware boards give way to brass and paper shops and the lanes narrow. West: the station forecourt and Paharganj (see `paharganj_station.md`). North along GB Road: the Lahori Gate and the Chandni Chowk side. First change: the building fronts (hardware boards to hotels or brass and paper), then the road width, then the lamp type.

## Map notes (P2-002)

Read from the photos; each item is a check against OSM and satellite.
- The Ajmeri Gate stands inside a fenced traffic island with the road passing on both sides (1 to 4). Check OSM does not run a road through the arch.
- Station forecourt apron about 8 to 10 m wide and kerbed with a yellow and black edge (15, 21, 22); auto and cab parking in rows (16, 17). Model it as a parking and apron area, not a road. A footbridge or flyover runs along the station front (17, 18); see the infrastructure variants.
- Hauz Qazi Chowk (23 to 27) is a multi-arm junction with a police booth and barricades. Check the number of arms against the photos and add the booth and barrier nodes.
- Delhi Gate and Turkman Gate (28 to 30) are gate-in-island pairs on the same wall line. Keep the same island model.
- Road width at the Ajmeri Gate about 10 to 12 m with a parking edge on both sides (1, 5 to 7); the effective lanes are two plus parking.
- Footpath: none to 1.5 m of broken concrete at the shops, 8 to 10 m apron at the station.
- Dividers: none in the photos at the gate road; a painted yellow and black kerb at the station edge (15, 21, 22).
