### What it is made of today

- **Changed since the old photos:** No big rebuild known since 2019. The 2022 Pinakpani series (Janpath, a road in New Delhi, and Tibet Market) is current and shows the road and the shopfronts at street level.
- **Facade colours (sampled):** k-means on the building band (18% to 52% of the frame height, sky, foliage, black and shadow removed, shown dark to light) of 10 street-level current photos (night shots skipped): #5D594F (25%), #8C7C56 (9%), #787C7C (13%), #A9A393 (12%), #CECBC3 (8%). Treat as the range; pick facade paints from these, and bright colours only where a photo shows them.
- **Road surface sampled:** the bottom centre of the same photos, largest colour cluster #797C86, others #4A484C, #AFABB5 (cars, paint and shadow are mixed in, so read the main value as the surface and judge it against the photos).
- Road surface and colour: dark grey asphalt (looks near black when wet), white lane lines and edge lines (seen, C-photos 2022).
- Kerbs: black and yellow painted concrete kerb stones along the median edge, grey stone kerb on the tree side (seen).
- Footpath: grey and red concrete pavers, about 3 m, with tree pits; the Tibet Market side has a red and white paver strip in front of the shops (seen).
- Median: raised median with black and yellow painted edges and planting (seen).
- Poles and wires: galvanised streetlight poles with cobra-head lamps, blue and green sign poles, few overhead wires (seen).
- Lights: streetlights at about 10 m spacing; Tibet Market has shop lights only (seen).
- Trees: dense canopy of mature trees (neem, rain tree) on both sides and in the median (seen).
- Signs: green boards "जनपथ JANPATH" in Hindi, English, Urdu with a left arrow; blue sign "TIBETAN MARKET" on a pole; shop fascia boards in red and white (seen).
- Typical clutter: parked scooters at the Tibet Market edge, hawker stands, jewellery stalls and stacked goods at shutters, autos and buses on the road (seen).

## Footpaths

Market frontage: a raised paved walk about 3 m wide with a red and cream chequer band along the shop line, roll-down shutters in front. Road side of the main road: grey paver footpath about 2.5 m with a yellow and black painted kerb stripe. Subways with polished stone floors and black stone bands at the stations.

## Road surface and markings

Dual carriageway: two lanes each way, a planted median about 2 m wide with a low black railing and painted yellow and black kerb. White dashed lane lines, a yellow double line at one stretch. Asphalt is good, mid-grey. The Tibetan Market service lane next to the shops is a narrow single lane, patched asphalt, leaf litter.

## Street lamps

Tall double-arm steel poles in the median with sodium lights. Small shop-side bulbs and fairy lights over the market at night. Metro sign is round red and blue with Hindi and English.

## Boundaries

Low black railing in the median. Green NDMC direction boards with white Hindi, Urdu and English. Shutters and a painted welcome wall at the market entrance. Office blocks along the east side have no front wall.

## Buildings

Tibetan Market: a long, low two storey row, flat roofed, with a red and cream painted band at the top and shop signs on the first floor. Handicraft and curio shops with open fronts. The office side: 4 to 6 storey concrete blocks with brand boards, AC units and shuttered windows. Many boards are in English only.

## Trees and planting

Large mature roadside trees (jamun, neem, peepal-like) forming a canopy over the road, fresh green. Dry fallen leaves on the market lane.

## Vendors and street life

Tourist market life: embroidered bags, bracelets, brass statues, Buddha figures, Tibetan curios. Sellers sit at open shutters or stand in front; tourists with bags. Night: lit stalls, bead strings, lights in the trees. A tiny cafe and juice sellers.

## Parking and vehicles

Autos and taxis along the service lane, motorbikes parked in the front of shops. The main road carries cars, buses, autos. Not many parked cars on the main carriageway.

## Typical clutter and wear

Wear 0.25. Shutters rolled and chipped, banners and tarps, dust and leaf litter. Boards are bright, some faded.

## What makes this area unlike the others

The only pilot area that is a straight tree-lined dual carriageway with a low row of craft shops at its side. Colours are a warm riot of hand-made cloth, beads and signs against grey roads and green trees. No colonnade, no lawns, no flyovers.

## Edge blending (what changes first)

North end: Janpath runs into the CP outer circle (colonnade ends, taller offices start). South end: it runs into the Lutyens zone (Rajpath cross-road area) and the road becomes a bungalow avenue; the market row stops first, then low walls and larger trees take over.
