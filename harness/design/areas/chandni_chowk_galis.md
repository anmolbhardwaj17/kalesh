### What it is made of today

- **Changed since the old photos:** Not rebuilt. The 2026 Old Delhi Bazaar series (C1 to C15) is the current look of the working lanes (old Delhi bazaar lanes; the series names Khari Baoli only for the last two, the other lanes are not named, so do not map them to a street yet). Old photos before 2019 are marked OLD.
- **Facade colours (sampled):** k-means on the building band (18% to 52% of the frame height, sky, foliage, black and shadow removed, shown dark to light) of 15 street-level current photos (night shots skipped): #5D5855 (26%), #7F7A75 (18%), #A89989 (12%), #C4B7AA (10%), #DCD3C7 (7%). Treat as the range; pick facade paints from these, and bright colours only where a photo shows them.
- **Road surface sampled:** the bottom centre of the same photos, largest colour cluster #B4A295, others #272221, #6E6158 (cars, paint and shadow are mixed in, so read the main value as the surface and judge it against the photos).
- Road surface and colour: patched, cracked asphalt and concrete, grey-brown, with standing puddles, rubble and spilled grain; no markings (seen, C1, C4).
- Kerbs: none; shopfronts open straight onto the carriageway. Some drain slabs and step edges in grey concrete (seen).
- Footpath: none in the narrow lanes; shop platforms (chabutra) and stacked sacks take the edge, about 0.5 to 1 m of raised shop floor (seen).
- Median: none.
- Poles and wires: concrete and steel poles carrying thick bundles of cables, junction boxes, transformers; wires cross at every storey (seen, C2, C6).
- Lights: a few bare tube lights and shop bulbs; very little street lighting (seen).
- Trees: rare; one old peepal or neem at a junction with a cow under it (seen).
- Signs: dense painted tin and flex signboards, Hindi and English, blue, white and red, three to four rows deep; shutters in green, blue, grey (seen).
- Typical clutter: handcarts, cycle rickshaws, e-rickshaws, sacks of grain and spice, gas cylinders, bicycles, cows and calves, concrete pipes stacked on the road (seen, C9, C10, C13).

## Footpaths

No raised footpath. The lane floor is one surface of old stone, brick or patched concrete, 2 to 4 m wide, stepped at shop thresholds. A drainage channel runs down one side. Surface worn and uneven, often wet.

## Road surface and markings

Concrete or old stone, sometimes patched with cement, dark and greasy near food stalls. No lane marking at all. Painted blue and white Delhi Government name boards at corners. Motorcycles and handcarts use the lane.

## Street lamps

Few real lamps: bare bulbs and tube lights on shop fronts, sodium lamps on a bracket only at lane mouths, neon and signs at night. A tangle of wires carries power.

## Boundaries

Roll-down shutters, wooden doors, iron gates for havelis, a narrow arch at lane entrances. Overhead tarps and cloth awnings in blue and white.

## Buildings

3 to 5 storeys, stepping outwards on cantilever balconies so the sky is a thin strip. Old haveli facades with cusped arches, jharokhas and painted walls in blue, cream and pink. Hand-painted shop boards in Urdu, Hindi and English. Roofs with water tanks and dishes.

## Trees and planting

None on the lane except a single peepal at a corner or a shrine. Plants only in pots on balconies.

## Vendors and street life

Extremely dense: porters with loads, shopkeepers on thresholds, sweet and snack makers frying in the lane, customers queuing, kids on balconies and washing lines, cats and dogs. Sound dominates.

## Parking and vehicles

Handcarts, tricycles, e-rickshaws, scooters in a single file. No cars.

## Typical clutter and wear

Wear 1.0. Soot, wire tangles, peeling paint, leaks, patched plaster, laundry, loose tarps, burnt oil walls.

## What makes this area unlike the others

Width. 2 to 4 m between 4 to 5 storey faces, with tangled wires and hanging signs. The only space that feels like an interior street. Its colour comes from awnings and washing, not from planting or wide light.

## Edge blending (what changes first)

Edges: lanes begin at the Chandni Chowk frontage as an arch gap in the shop row; the first 15 m are wider and brighter, then walls close in. They continue north into the Kinari Bazar and Dariba Kalan market and west toward Jama Masjid road.

## Street-level top-up (P2-002, 2026-11-13)

Where this section differs from an older line above, this section wins. Tile numbers are board tiles.

**Footpaths.** There is none. One worn surface of stone, brick or concrete from shutter to shutter, with an open drain along one edge (31, 32). Shops spill 0.5 to 1.5 m into the lane with stools, steps, stalls and hanging stock (27, 28, 30, 34). Raised thresholds at doors, no kerbs.

**Road surface and markings.** No markings. Patched concrete, stone setts or asphalt, wet patches and spilled water (31, 32, 38). Widths read by scale: Ballimaran and Katra Bariyan about 5 to 6 m (27 to 29, 32, 33), the bazaar lanes about 4 m (34), small galis 2.5 to 3.5 m (31, 35), Khari Baoli and Chawri Bazar about 8 to 10 m with carts parked on both sides (30, 44 to 46).

**Street lamps.** No poles in the lanes. Bare bulbs, tube lights and string lights on the shopfronts and awnings (34, 41). Lit signboards. At the wider crossings a single pole with a curved arm (36, 38).

**Boundaries.** Rolled steel shutters in many colours (28, 31, 33, 39), haveli iron gates and arched doorways with carved plaster (35, 47), arches across lane mouths (31, 40), tarpaulins and awnings in blue, green and orange (26, 32, 34).

**Buildings.** 3 to 5 storeys, shop below and home or godown above. Cantilevered balconies and jharokhas, painted haveli faces in blue, ochre, pink and cream, lime plaster, exposed brick, rooftop tanks, hoardings and signboards in Hindi, Urdu and English (28, 29, 33, 42). A courtyard haveli has blue lime plaster, arched niches and exposed conduit (35). A carved doorway in a teal door with floral plaster surround (47).

**Trees and planting.** Almost none inside the lanes. A neem or peepal at a crossing or a temple (37, 38). Potted plants at a doorstep. Rooftop pigeons.

**Vendors and street life.** Handcarts, porters, hawkers with a tray or a cart, cycle rickshaws, tea and chaat stalls, shoppers in all kinds of clothing, schoolchildren, older men sitting on steps, shopkeepers on stools (26 to 34, 41, 44). In the evening the lanes fill with warm light and a dense crowd (41). Some lanes are quiet with the shutters down in the early morning (31, 39).

**Parking and vehicles.** Cycle rickshaws, handcarts, motorbikes parked in rows, e-rickshaws, autos at the wider lanes, a car at the lane mouth (28, 29, 36, 37, 43, 48). Little in the narrowest galis.

**Typical clutter and wear.** Heavy: tangled overhead wires and cable bundles above the lanes (28, 29, 33, 39), meters and conduits on walls (35), stacked cartons and sacks, awnings, hanging signs, spilled water, litter. Wear 1.0 is confirmed, with 0.9 in the wider market lanes.

**What sets it apart (added).** The canopy of wires and awnings, a lane width that changes every few metres, and constant movement of people. At lane level you almost never see the sky.

## Map notes (P2-002)

- Side-lane mouths open about every 30 to 60 m along the market streets (28, 33, 42). Many of these are likely missing from OSM; check against satellite and add the ones wider than 2.5 m.
- Archways over lane mouths (31, 40): gateway objects about 3 to 4 m wide and 4 to 5 m tall.
- Widths: Ballimaran and Katra Bariyan about 5 to 6 m, bazaar lanes about 4 m, small galis 2.5 to 3.5 m, Khari Baoli and Chawri Bazar about 8 to 10 m before parking.
