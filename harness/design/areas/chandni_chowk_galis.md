## Footpaths

No raised footpath. The lane floor is one surface of old stone, brick or patched concrete, 2 to 4 m wide, stepped at shop thresholds. A drainage channel runs down one side. Surface worn and uneven, often wet.

## Road surface and markings

Concrete or old stone, sometimes patched with cement, dark and greasy near food stalls. No lane marking at all. Painted blue and white Delhi Government name boards at corners. Motorcycles and handcarts use the lane.

## Street lamps

Few real lamps: bare bulbs and tube lights on shop fronts, sodium lamps on a bracket only at lane mouths, neon and signs at night. A tangle of wires carries power.

## Boundaries

Roll-down shutters, wooden doors, iron gates for havelis, a narrow arch at lane entrances. Overhead tarps and cloth awnings in blue and white.

## Buildings

3 to 5 storeys, stepping outwards on cantilever balconies so the sky is a thin strip. Old haveli facades with cusped arches, jharokhas and painted walls in blue, cream and pink. Hand-painted shop boards in Urdu, Hindi and English. Roofs with water tanks and dishes.

## Trees and planting

None on the lane except a single peepal at a corner or a shrine. Plants only in pots on balconies.

## Vendors and street life

Extremely dense: porters with loads, shopkeepers on thresholds, sweet and snack makers frying in the lane, customers queuing, kids on balconies and washing lines, cats and dogs. Sound dominates.

## Parking and vehicles

Handcarts, tricycles, e-rickshaws, scooters in a single file. No cars.

## Typical clutter and wear

Wear 1.0. Soot, wire tangles, peeling paint, leaks, patched plaster, laundry, loose tarps, burnt oil walls.

## What makes this area unlike the others

Width. 2 to 4 m between 4 to 5 storey faces, with tangled wires and hanging signs. The only space that feels like an interior street. Its colour comes from awnings and washing, not from planting or wide light.

## Edge blending (what changes first)

Edges: lanes begin at the Chandni Chowk frontage as an arch gap in the shop row; the first 15 m are wider and brighter, then walls close in. They continue north into the Kinari Bazar and Dariba Kalan market and west toward Jama Masjid road.
