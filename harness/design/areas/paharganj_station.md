### What it is made of today

- **Changed since the old photos:** No rebuild of Main Bazar known since 2019. The New Delhi station Ajmeri Gate side is current from 2022 (see the G.B. Road sheet). Main Bazar photos from 2020 are lockdown-empty and show shopfront colours well; 2019 and 2024 night shots show the crowd and lights.
- **Facade colours (sampled):** k-means on the building band (18% to 52% of the frame height, sky, foliage, black and shadow removed, shown dark to light) of 12 street-level current photos (night shots skipped): #615D5E (20%), #A36F42 (12%), #8C817D (15%), #C4AB98 (14%). Treat as the range; pick facade paints from these, and bright colours only where a photo shows them.
- **Road surface sampled:** the bottom centre of the same photos, largest colour cluster #686359, others #2D231F, #CBC2B4 (cars, paint and shadow are mixed in, so read the main value as the surface and judge it against the photos).
- Road surface and colour: worn grey asphalt and concrete, patched, wet at times (seen, 2020 empty road shots).
- Kerbs: low, broken concrete kerbs; no markings (seen).
- Footpath: narrow, shared with stalls and parked scooters; grey tile in places (seen).
- Median: none on Main Bazar; the station road has a painted median (seen).
- Poles and wires: tangled overhead wires at every storey, transformers on poles, bundled cables over the lane (seen, 2019 evening).
- Lights: strings of fairy lights on whole facades, neon hotel signs (vertical, red and yellow), tube lights over stalls (seen, Diwali 2024 shots).
- Trees: very few; a tree at the station road corner (seen).
- Signs: vertical neon hotel boards, painted shop fascias with Hindi and English, shutters with painted names (seen).
- Typical clutter: hawkers, handcarts, e-rickshaws, tuk-tuks, backpackers, scooters on the pavement, cows (seen).

## Footpaths

Main Bazaar: no real footpath; shops spill onto 1 m of broken concrete or paver at the shopfront. Station side: a wide forecourt with grey granite tile and railings. DB Gupta Road: 2 m footpath with kerbs, uneven.

## Road surface and markings

Main Bazaar is about 7 to 8 m wide, patched asphalt or concrete, no lane lines. DB Gupta Road is a four-lane one-way road with painted dashes. Under the station flyover the road is a wide junction with faded lines.

## Street lamps

Bare bulbs and tube lights at shopfronts, a few street poles with sodium heads, neon from hotel signs and hoardings. Large lit hoardings on the roof of hotels.

## Boundaries

Roll-down shutters in many colours (blue shutters), iron gates, narrow arch entrances for old lanes. Metal railings at the station. Walls of old havelis with brackets.

## Buildings

Dense, tall 4 to 6 storey buildings jammed edge to edge, painted pink, blue, yellow; large hotel and guesthouse signs stacked on every floor, rooftop terraces with cell masts, balconies, dish antennas and a rooftop mosque or temple. Old haveli fragments with carved brackets. Backpacker signs in English and Hebrew. The station: a long brown and cream concourse with a flyover and metal canopy.

## Trees and planting

Very little: a few peepal or neem at corners and roofs, potted plants on terraces. The station has a few trees.

## Vendors and street life

Backpackers, shopkeepers, porters, rickshaw pullers, fruit sellers, tailor shops, Tibetan monks, cows in the lane, tourists. Day and night market.

## Parking and vehicles

Cycle and e-rickshaws, motorbikes, autos, handcarts; cars at the station side; tour vans and taxis queue at the Ajmeri Gate exit. Buses at the flyover.

## Typical clutter and wear

Wear 0.8. Wires, garlands, tarpaulins, stained plaster, hanging signs, litter, cows, burnt oil, graffiti.

## What makes this area unlike the others

The backpacker bazaar and rail hub: international signs, hotel stacks, a station next door, trains behind the roofs, tourists mixed with locals, bright hoardings. No other pilot area has a rail terminal or foreign-language signs.

## Edge blending (what changes first)

Edges: east side meets the station and Ajmeri Gate; north-east joins the Chandni Chowk / Old Delhi side via Asaf Ali Road; south meets Karol Bagh and Connaught Place. First change: hotel stacks reduce to 3 to 4 floor shops and the bazaar widens, then big hoardings go.


## Station forecourt, Ajmeri Gate side (P2-002, 2026-11-13)

Added from the GB Road and Ajmeri Gate sheet (`gb_road_ajmeri_gate.md`, tiles 12 to 22 there). The forecourt is a wide asphalt apron about 8 to 10 m deep in front of a long two-storey colonnade with white columns, a red brick base and painted wrap panels along the upper floor, bounded by a yellow and black kerb (tiles 15, 21, 22 there). Autos in green and yellow, e-rickshaws and white cabs queue in rows four to six wide, and hundreds fill it at peak (16, 17). The entrance has a large arch sign over the road with a parking notice (18). A tall mast carries a cluster of lamp heads (16, 17, 18). The Paharganj entrance on the other side is an orange multi-storey block with coloured panels (20). Treat the forecourt as a parking and apron polygon, not a street. Confidence: medium. Map notes: the apron edge is a kerb, not a wall; auto bays are painted rows; a footbridge runs along the front.
